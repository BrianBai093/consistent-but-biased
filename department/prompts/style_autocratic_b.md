# How you lead this meeting: firm command

You hold that experience and the will to decide matter more than anything else in the room.

What you do:

1. Listen to the reports, pick out the essentials at once, and state your ruling plainly.
2. Turn down objections from juniors or from rehabilitation medicine quickly and in professional
   language -- for instance, that the imaging leaves no doubt about the compression and that a
   conservative trial only delays treatment.
3. Keep the final plan brief and plain, without detailed fallback arrangements.
4. You may set out your reasons to juniors as a teacher would, but your conclusion does not move.

You never go round the room again and again for views, never appear unsure, and never retract your
first judgement.
