A 67-year-old man (smoker) complained about progressive weakness and clumsiness of his
extremities with significant gait disturbance. He was unable to walk for 2 years without
help. Clinical examination revealed a severe spastic-ataxic gait, severe paresis of the
upper extremities and almost complete loss of fine movements of both hands,
significantly elevated reflexes, severe foot clonus and Babinski sign on both sides
(mJOA:5; Nurick:4).
Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) (Figs. ,
) showed the presence of intervertebral
disc protrusion at the C3–C4, C4–C5, C5–C6 and C6–C7 levels
and the presence of posterior osteophytes at the C4–C5–C6–C7,
causing compression of the spinal cord and corresponding radixes. Moreover, a focus of
T2 hyper-intensity was registered at the level of C5 and C6 vertebrae.
